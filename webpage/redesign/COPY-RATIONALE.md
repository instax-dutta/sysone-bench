# Public copy rationale

Purpose: make the site read as a decision tool, not a research log, without changing or overstating
any measurement.

## Research

Comparable independent benchmarks lead with what the reader can decide, then prove methodology:

- Artificial Analysis: "Comparison and ranking the performance of over 250 AI models ... across key
  metrics including intelligence, price, performance and speed."
- Epoch AI: "Our database of benchmark results ... Explore trends in AI capabilities across time, by
  benchmark, or by model."
- LiveBench: "Overall + every suite, sortable. Click a row for its per-suite detail." and notes that
  "a score alone doesn't tell you whether a model is worth it."

Pattern: outcome first, method second, one obvious next action.

## Mental models applied

- **Jobs to Be Done**: the visitor hires this site to shortlist a decision model, not to read a lab
  notebook. The hero names the job: "Choose with evidence, not claims."
- **Curse of Knowledge**: "sealed manifest" and "noul" are moved out of the first paragraph. The lede
  uses plain verbs: run ourselves, see, check, take.
- **Framing / Contrast Effect**: "not claims" contrasts our measurements with vendor-published scores
  without attacking any vendor.
- **Authority Bias**: "independent benchmark", "same sealed cases", "pinned revisions" and "our
  measurements" establish who measured what, immediately.
- **Pratfall Effect**: the caveats about top-band ties, single-reviewed gold and readout methods stay
  prominent; admitting limits increases trust.
- **Hick's Law / Activation Energy**: one primary action, "Explore the field", with a quiet secondary
  "Download the data". No competing CTAs.
- **Peak-End Rule**: the footer closes with "Use it to shortlist a model, then verify the choice on
  your own data."

No count, score, digest or model name was hand-typed into the new copy. All volatile figures remain
generated from `data/results.json`.
